Marketing Case Clinic: 17 Scenarios Solved

One three-line scenario for each seminar topic, each solved with the same clear structure: diagnosis, framework, moves and risk.

1. The Eras: The Quota Factory

Scenario

A furniture chain says it is “customer-obsessed,” yet its weekly dashboard shows only units sold and calls made per rep. Bonuses reward volume, and returns are rising. The CEO asks which era the firm truly lives in.

Diagnosis

It is a Sales Era firm wearing Marketing Era language; metrics reveal culture.

Framework at Work

Marketing Era firms track lifetime value, retention and share of wallet.

Recommended Moves

Add repeat-purchase rate and return reasons to the dashboard, and tie bonuses partly to customer retention.

Risk to Watch

Reps may resist lower short-term commissions, so phase the change in.

2. Customization Ladder: The Sneaker Studio

Scenario

A shoe brand offers ten fixed colorways online. Fans keep asking to design their own pair, while the data team wants auto-suggested styles. Leadership must choose a path.

Diagnosis

The brand sits at customization and faces demand for customerisation.

Framework at Work

Customization means the firm sets options, customerisation means the customer co-creates, and personalization means data-driven tailoring.

Recommended Moves

Launch a design-your-own tool for premium lines, and use personalization only for recommendations.

Risk to Watch

Over-tracking can feel creepy; give customers visible control of their data.

3. Purple Cow: The Forgettable Café

Scenario

A new café serves good coffee at fair prices, just like six others on the same street. Foot traffic is thin and nobody posts about it. The owner has a small budget and no story.

Diagnosis

It is a brown cow: good, but not remarkable.

Framework at Work

Godin says remarkable means worth remarking on, and safe copying guarantees invisibility.

Recommended Moves

Pick one bold twist, such as a silent-reading café or a coffee-flight tasting bar, and build everything around it.

Risk to Watch

A gimmick with weak coffee fails fast; remarkability must sit on top of quality.

4. Competitor, Alternate, Substitute: The Cinema Chain

Scenario

A regional cinema chain benchmarks only against two rival cinemas. Its ticket sales keep sliding anyway. Teenagers spend their evenings on streaming, games and short videos.

Diagnosis

The chain watches competitors and ignores alternates and substitutes.

Framework at Work

A substitute meets a different need but can still displace you, and it is often the real threat.

Recommended Moves

Define the need as “a shared evening out” and design events, premium seating and group packages around it.

Risk to Watch

Redrawing the map is useless if it is not repeated every year.

5. Skimming vs. Penetration: The Smart Ring

Scenario

A startup has patented a health-tracking ring that rivals cannot copy for three years. Early fans pay almost anything, yet investors want rapid market share. The founders debate the launch price.

Diagnosis

Strong IP and eager early adopters point to skimming.

Framework at Work

Skimming works with novelty; penetration works when scale and switching costs matter.

Recommended Moves

Launch high, then cascade down in stages while building a subscription service for later profit.

Risk to Watch

If the patent weakens, the high price invites undercutting.

6. Price Tactics: The Gym Membership

Scenario

A gym sells a $30 basic plan and a $60 premium plan, but most people pick basic. Managers add a $55 plan with fewer perks than premium. Premium sign-ups jump.

Diagnosis

The $55 plan works as a decoy.

Framework at Work

Asymmetric dominance makes the target option look like an obvious bargain.

Recommended Moves

Keep the decoy honest, with real differences that are clearly explained.

Risk to Watch

If customers feel tricked, trust and retention suffer.

7. Channel Strategy: The Skincare Brand

Scenario

A skincare brand sells only through a large marketplace that owns customer data and sets prices. Margins are shrinking and reviews are out of its control. The founder considers building its own website and stores.

Diagnosis

The brand is renting its customers from the marketplace.

Framework at Work

The key question is who owns the relationship and the last mile of experience.

Recommended Moves

Build a direct site for first-party data while keeping the marketplace for reach.

Risk to Watch

Going fully direct can cut visibility and shelf presence.

8. Promotion Mix: The Energy Drink

Scenario

A small energy drink brand spends its whole budget on banner ads that nobody clicks. Meanwhile local skate and climbing communities are asking for sponsors. The marketing lead needs a new plan.

Diagnosis

Promotion has no clear job and relies on interruption.

Framework at Work

Experiential, community and earned media invite participation instead of demanding attention.

Recommended Moves

Sponsor local events, film the best moments and share them as owned content.

Risk to Watch

Without a defined goal such as trial or community, even a fun event is noise.

9. Services 3 Ps: The Dental Clinic

Scenario

A dental clinic advertises “pain-free care,” but patients wait long and the waiting room looks tired. Receptionists seem rushed and rude. Online reviews are turning negative.

Diagnosis

The promise is not backed by People, Process and Physical Evidence.

Framework at Work

Customers judge intangible services by the staff, the workflow and visible cues.

Recommended Moves

Train staff, add appointment reminders and fix the clinic’s look and cleanliness.

Risk to Watch

Better ads before better delivery will only speed up bad word of mouth.

10. Post-Purchase: The Online Shoe Store

Scenario

An online shoe store gets plenty of first orders, but few customers return. Returns are costly and confusing. Service replies take three days.

Diagnosis

The store treats the sale as the finish line.

Framework at Work

The first three days decide remorse, and the first thirty decide habit or churn.

Recommended Moves

Offer easy returns, a quick check-in message and fast, empowered service.

Risk to Watch

Generous returns need cost control, such as better size guidance.

11. Buying Center: The Hospital Software

Scenario

A vendor sells scheduling software to hospitals and pitches only the IT head. Doctors find the demo confusing and the finance office questions the price. Deals keep stalling.

Diagnosis

The vendor addressed one person in a multi-role decision unit.

Framework at Work

Initiators, influencers, deciders, buyers and users each need a tailored message.

Recommended Moves

Show doctors time savings, show finance ROI, and show IT security and integration.

Risk to Watch

Roles shift between organizations, so remap each account.

12. Segmentation: The Meal Kit

Scenario

A meal-kit company splits customers by age and city, but sends everyone the same discounts. Some order weekly, others once and vanish. Marketing spend keeps rising.

Diagnosis

The segments exist but do not change any decision.

Framework at Work

Behavioral segmentation, based on actions, is the most useful for prompting action.

Recommended Moves

Reward weekly loyalists, nudge occasional buyers and run win-back offers for lapsed ones.

Risk to Watch

Too many micro-segments become expensive theater.

13. Life Cycle and Adoption: The Electric Scooter

Scenario

An e-scooter startup won over tech lovers with specs and speed. Mainstream commuters now hesitate over safety, battery life and repairs. Growth is flattening.

Diagnosis

The firm must cross from early adopters to the early majority.

Framework at Work

Strategy must shift with both life-cycle stage and adopter type.

Recommended Moves

Lead with safety ratings, warranties, service points and commuter testimonials.

Risk to Watch

Dropping the original message too fast may alienate loyal fans.

14. Positioning: The Muddled Bank

Scenario

A bank advertises itself as cheap, premium, innovative and traditional all at once. Surveys show customers cannot say what it stands for. Rivals clearly own “low fees” and “digital.”

Diagnosis

The positioning is wallpaper: neither distinctive nor believed.

Framework at Work

Positioning is the belief in the customer’s mind, not the message sent.

Recommended Moves

Choose one important dimension, such as trusted advice for families, and prove it consistently.

Risk to Watch

Claims need credible proof, or customers reject them.

15. Law of Mortality: The Printing Firm

Scenario

A printing company earns most of its profit from paper brochures, which fall by 10% each year. Managers avoid discussing digital services to protect current margins. A competitor is already moving online.

Diagnosis

Management is in denial about decline.

Framework at Work

Every product eventually declines; the choice is harvest, revitalize or exit early.

Recommended Moves

Harvest brochures for cash, and invest in packaging and web-to-print services.

Risk to Watch

Late action leaves only emergency options.

16. Buying Process: The Cola Challenger

Scenario

A new cola has a great taste test score but little shelf success. Shoppers grab their usual brand without thinking. Price cuts barely move sales.

Diagnosis

The challenger faces habitual repurchase, not a full evaluation.

Framework at Work

Choice trades off prestige, quality, time, effort and price, so price is rarely the only lever.

Recommended Moves

Use sampling, eye-level placement and a trigger moment that forces fresh comparison.

Risk to Watch

Breaking a loop costs more than building one, so budget accordingly.

17. Ansoff Matrix: The Bakery Chain

Scenario

A successful bakery chain wants to open a restaurant and launch a fashion line. Its current stores still have weak weekday sales. Investors are excited by the bold plan.

Diagnosis

Management is romanticizing diversification and neglecting penetration.

Framework at Work

Diversification, with new products and new markets, is the riskiest cell.

Recommended Moves

Grow weekday sales with loyalty offers, then try product development such as catering.

Risk to Watch

Diversify only with a real capability advantage and a learning mindset.
